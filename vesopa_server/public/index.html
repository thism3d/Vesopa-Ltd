Staff

The people who work the till. They sign on at the terminal with a PIN, not an email — and whoever is signed on is the name that prints as “Served by”. Permissions and rota will be added to this section later.

NameIDPINStatus

PINs must be exactly 4 digits and no two people can share one — the till pad signs someone on the moment the fourth digit lands, so a longer PIN could never be used and a shared one would attribute sales to whoever was found first.

Till & printers

What every terminal in this venue does between customers, and what the venue calls each printer.

Background

Leave this empty for the built-in Vesopa screen, which is drawn on the terminal rather than loaded — so it always renders, even on a till with no network.

When it appears

Turn the PIN off for a fast counter where one entry per customer costs more than the attribution is worth — the idle screen then clears on any touch.

Automatic sign off

0 turns it off. Anything from 20 to 3600 seconds is accepted; 180 (three minutes) is the default. This never closes the cash session and never clears the bill on screen — whoever signs back on picks it up exactly where it was left.

Change window

After a sale settles the till shows what to hand back, then counts down. When it reaches zero it signs the staff member off and drops to the idle screen, so the terminal is ready for the next customer without anyone touching it. 30 seconds is the default; 5 to 300 is accepted. 0 leaves the box up until it is tapped.

Receipt printing

The till no longer asks the clerk whether the customer wants a receipt — that question was being put over the head of the next person in the queue, several hundred times a day, and the answer is the same every time in any given venue. Leave this off and no paper comes out at the counter; staff print one on request from the Receipts screen or the Last Bill key.

Printer names

What this venue calls each printer. These names appear on the product editor, in the catalogue, and on every till — so a station called “Fryer” here is “Fryer” everywhere. Leave one blank to use its standard name. Which printer is physically plugged in where is set on each till, under Settings › Printing.

The receipt printer is a routing destination too: tick it on a product and the counter gets its own ticket for that item, as well as the kitchen getting theirs.

Product buttons

On by default. Turn it off for a cleaner grid where staff know the menu, or where prices change often enough that the button is not where anyone should be reading them. The price still shows on the bill as soon as the item is rung up.

Kitchen screens

Vesopa Kitchen shows a station its orders on a touch screen instead of printing them. Products are still routed to KP 1 to KP 6 in the product editor exactly as they are now — this decides where each station’s ticket comes out.

Where each station delivers

Every station starts on Printer, which is what your tills do today — nothing changes until you move one. Both is worth running for a week or two before you unplug anything.

Which printer is physically plugged in where is still set on each till, under Settings › Printing — that is a fact about a counter, not about the venue. A station set to Screen needs no printer at all.

Kitchen logins

What gets typed into the screen on the wall. These belong to the screen, not to a member of staff — one per kitchen is usually right. Keep the username short: it is typed on glass with a finger.

Screens

A named board: which stations it shows, and how it behaves. Each machine picks which of these it is when it is signed in, so “the grill screen” means one thing in the venue and you can change it from here without touching the wall.

A kitchen with one screen needs none of this — an unconfigured screen shows every station, which is the right answer for them.

Start screen & branding

What the screens in this venue call themselves, and what they show while they start up. Leave a box empty and the screen falls back — to your receipt logo, then to the built-in Vesopa mark. This applies to every kitchen screen on the site, and takes effect on each one within a few seconds.

No logo

PNG with a transparent background works best. Shown on glass at about 120 px, so a mark reads better than a wordmark.

0 lets it clear the moment the board is ready. The board is fetched behind the start screen either way, so this never delays the orders arriving — it only decides how long they wait to be looked at. Six seconds is the most allowed.

Screen programming

Lay out what your tills show. The sale screen is a grid of buttons — each one a product, a jump to another screen, or a till function — and the two strips of chrome around it are laid out the same way: the top bar that carries your open tables, and the bottom bar that carries Pay.

The editor is open in its own window

Screen programming wants the whole screen — a grid, an inspector beside it and a till preview under it — so it opens in a window of its own. Look for the Vesopa screen editor window behind this one.

“Edit in this tab instead” is remembered on this computer. The ⤢ Own window key above puts it back.

Double-click a key to search for what it should be — any product, page or till function, by name. Drag the handle in its corner to make it bigger or smaller. Click a key to select it, or drag a box across several. Shift extends the box, Ctrl adds one at a time. To move keys, select them and then drag the selection — hold Alt to copy instead. Arrow keys walk the grid, Alt+arrows nudge what is selected, and Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+C, Ctrl+V, Ctrl+D and Ctrl+S do what they do everywhere else. Backspace clears the selection.

Fonts

What your tills letter everything in. A button can be given a font of its own; the rest follow this one.

TrueType (.ttf) or OpenType (.otf). A .woff2 works in a browser but not on a till, so it is refused rather than quietly ignored. Uploading a font is you saying you hold a licence to install it on your tills.
