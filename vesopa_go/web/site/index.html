Inside the app

Every step of the payment, on screen.

The same five steps as the Windows till: starting, present card, PIN, processing, complete. The customer sees where to hold their card, and the clerk sees exactly where the payment has got to.

Step 1 of 5

  1. 01

    Take the order

    The till's own buttons and colours, on the phone. Tap, add a note, choose the options the kitchen needs.

  2. 02

    Check the bill

    Seats, deals and discounts, exactly as the till works them out. Split it, print it, or send it to the till.

  3. 03

    Choose how to pay

    Cash, Tap to Pay, the venue's card machine, QR Pay, a gift card or a voucher: the till's own pay screen.

  4. 04

    Or let them scan

    QR Pay puts the bill on the customer's own phone, with every item listed, and closes it the moment it's paid.

  5. 05

    Done, and in the books

    Approved in seconds. The sale is in the back office at once, with the receipt printed, emailed or shown.

Payments

Three ways to take the money, none of them a queue.

  • Tap to Pay on iPhoneThe customer holds their contactless card, iPhone or other digital wallet at the top of your iPhone. No extra hardware, with Dojo.
  • QR PayA code on the screen or the printed bill. The customer scans it and pays on their own phone by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay; the bill closes itself.
  • The venue's card machineSend the amount to the venue's Dojo or Teya machine from any device, with tips and split bills.

The venue network

When the Wi-Fi fails, the till becomes the Wi-Fi.

Go, the tills, the kitchen screens and the customer displays talk over the venue's own network, encrypted, so tables move in milliseconds. If the venue Wi-Fi drops or puts phones on a separate network, the Windows till can share its own network as a hotspot from Settings › Network and hardware, and the phones join it.

The same page shows every adapter, Bluetooth and USB device on the till, with warnings and restarts, and Go shows its own connection, the response time and the tills it can see.
